How ModelCheck works From prediction files to an experiment you approve.

Use ModelCheck to understand where a binary classifier succeeds, where it misses examples, and what to investigate next. Start with the synthetic samples or bring prediction exports with labels 0 and 1.

  1. Bring your results

    The manifest describes each example, its true label, split and source. Add baseline predictions and, optionally, candidate predictions to compare two runs.

  2. Check the measured evidence

    Python tools check coverage and data issues, calculate metrics, and inspect source slices. The Model Context Protocol (MCP) connects the reviewer to these tools. Open Activity & evidence to see the calculations behind a finding.

  3. Ask the review agent

    Ask a question such as “Where did recall get worse?” The agent follows a review checklist, chooses read-only tools, and cites their evidence in its findings. Model calls and tool use have execution limits.

  4. Choose the next experiment

    Draft a follow-up with one change and a success criterion based on validation data. Review the exact specification: approval saves a downloadable JSON plan; rejection saves no file. Training is a separate step.

Read the metrics

Accuracy
The share of test examples classified correctly.
Positive recall
The share of actual positives the model finds. Lower recall means more missed positives, or false negatives.
F1 score
A combined measure of precision and recall. Precision is the share of positive predictions that are correct.

Try the quantization sample: overall accuracy falls from 90% to 85%, while web recall falls from 90% to 50%. Source slices reveal a much larger change than the headline score.

Data and interpretation: use public or synthetic exports. Files and saved plans stay local; live AI review sends your question, bounded tool results and run descriptions to OpenRouter. Source differences are descriptive, and exported predictions cannot verify training or preprocessing claims.
